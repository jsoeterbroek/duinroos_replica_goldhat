# Catalogue: Wikipedia + museum synthesis (how it was checked)

**What it is:** the public-facing claims, to stop the project re-importing them as
if they were primary.

**How checked:** **search-inside** of the raw wikitext of *Berlin Gold Hat*,
*Gold hat* (en) and *Berliner Goldhut*, *Goldhut* (de); the museum text is quoted
in `sources/wikipedia_and_museum.md`. Search-inside is adequate for named claims
(the text is short) but **not** for the bibliography completeness.

## What the public synthesis claims

- 21 ornamental zones; ~490 g; 745 mm; 89.7 % gold; 0.6 mm thick.
- A lunisolar calendar: 354 lunar days, 365 solar days, 7 intercalary months in
  19 years, even eclipse prediction (Neues Museum text, via Google Arts & Culture).
- The 54-month example `1739 − 142 = 1597` and intercalary zones {5,7,16,17}
  (transcribed from Menghin 2000).

## Discrepancies with the primary sources

| Public claim | Primary source |
|---|---|
| intercalary {5,7,16,17} | 2010 figs use {7,16,17} |
| "~3,393 symbols" (in the digest) | not a count of anything |
| Ezelsdorf "1739 symbols" | ≈ 499 symbols / ≈ 1761 rings |
| "eclipse prediction" | not in Menghin's ornament analysis |

## Use

The museum text is the *claim under test*, not evidence. Cite it as "the museum's
reading". Its strength is that it shows the hypothesis has institutional currency;
its weakness is that it is a secondary synthesis.

## Limits

Search-inside on Wikipedia cannot tell us what the cited print sources say beyond
the quoted passages; where a claim matters, go to the print source.
