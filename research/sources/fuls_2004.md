# Source: Fuls 2004 — Goldhüte and Ostfriesland (ORDO ET MENSURA VIII)

**What it is:** Andreas Fuls, *Archäoastronomische Anmerkungen zu bronzezeitlichen
Goldhüten und ostfriesischen Kultbauten*, in: F. Huber & R. C. A. Rottländer (eds),
*ORDO ET MENSURA VIII*, Scripta Mercaturae, St. Katharinen 2004, pp. 39–57.
A quantitative re-analysis of all four gold hats that keeps Menghin's day-value
unit but **requires zone groups to share a decoration type**, then adds correction
cycles. This is the published, independent version of the selectivity test.

**Licence:** in copyright. The author has posted the PDF openly.

## Key content (verified on the page image)

- **Schifferstadt:** zones 3–8 = 266 circles ≈ 9 synodic months; zones 2–9 =
  355 ≈ 12 synodic months; correction cycles of 92 × 266 − 22 and 110 × 266 − 24.
- **Avanton:** zones 12–14 = 177 d ≈ 6 months (−0.184 d); 46 × 104 = 4784 d =
  162 months +0.045 d.
- **Berlin:** zones 9, 11, 13 (type 12) = 59; zones 10, 12, 14 (type 14) = 60 —
  the rounded double month; zones 4+6+8+7+16 = 365; a 15 × 59 + 60 = 945-day
  cycle, 47 × 945 with a 944-day correction giving a mean month of
  **29.530585 d** ("unglaubliche Genauigkeit", but he himself doubts it); a
  97 155-day / 266-year solar cycle.
- **Ezelsdorf:** of the cycles tested only zones 6–21 = 1461 d (4 yr) and 1418 d
  (48 months) are "genau"; most are "ungenau" or "fragwürdig".
- **Conclusion:** all four hats plus the Ostfriesland post-rows and Nebra share a
  common Central European astronomical knowledge; an east→west simple→complex
  sequence (Avanton, Schifferstadt → Ezelsdorf, Berlin).

## Known traps in this source

- Fuls's method *reduces* the search space (zone groups must share a symbol type),
  which is stricter than Menghin — but he still allows the group boundaries to be
  read off the ornament, so it is not fully pre-registered.
- He reports his own Berlin result as **"muss deswegen in Frage gestellt werden"**
  (the 29.530585 d month). Quote the doubt with the number.
- His Ostfriesland post-row argument is a separate, landscape-based claim.
