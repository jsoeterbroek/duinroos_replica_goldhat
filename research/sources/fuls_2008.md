# Source: Fuls 2008 — the statistical null test (Nuncius Hamburgensis 8)

**What it is:** Andreas Fuls, *Astronomisch-statistische Analyse von Kreissymbolen
bronzezeitlicher Goldhüte*, in: G. Wolfschmidt (ed.), *Prähistorische Astronomie
und Ethnoastronomie* (Nuncius Hamburgensis 8), Norderstedt 2008, pp. 113–129.
The paper builds the explicit combinatorial null for all four gold hats and tests
the *frequency* of astronomical hits against random expectation.

## Key content (verified on the page image)

- Four combination methods: **A** arbitrary zone subsets, **B** groups of zones
  with the same symbol type, **C** ordered contiguous zones, **D** ordered zones
  minus switch-zones. For Berlin, G = 1 048 575 (A), 1023 (B), 210 (C), 699 (D).
- Compare the hit frequency `h = T/G` with the chance probability
  `w = 2·t_p/p · 100 %` (tolerance `t_p` from Table 8.1).
- **Result:** "in fast allen Fällen die relative Häufigkeit der Treffer genau der
  Wahrscheinlichkeit einer zufälligen Auswahl entspricht, d. h. h ≈ w". Only
  **Schifferstadt** is significant: method B × synodic month = 16.13 % vs 6.77 %
  expected (factor 2.1 at t ≈ 0.3 d), confirmed by its metrology (3:9 and 9:15
  ratios). For **Berlin** the hit rate tracks the random line; the tropical year
  is 0 % (C) and 0.43 % (D).
- He explicitly warns the *opposite* way too: with G up to 65 535 for Ezelsdorf/
  Berlin, a real encoded ratio can be drowned out. "Eine statistische Untersuchung
  allein kann letztendlich nicht entscheiden."

## Known traps in this source

- The method treats **all zone combinations as equally likely**; it tests
  *frequency*, not the arrangement. A designed-but-sparse encoding could still
  hide. He says so.
- He also re-tests planetary periods (Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn) —
  relevant to Schmidt-Kaler: none are significant for Berlin.
