  Bronzezeit*, Acta Praehistorica et Archaeologica 32 (2000), 31–108.

## Readability (asked 2026-10-12)

Workable, and good enough for the pipeline. Per page:

- **Text layer:** present on every page (an OCR layer made by the scanning app,
  ~27 000 characters per PDF). Body text is clean and searchable. It is **jumbled
  in the tables and multi-column figure captions** — the numbers of a table come
  out in reading-order fragments (`71О`, `Зх8` with Cyrillic look-alikes, `‡` for
  `±`). Use it for grep, never for a number.
- **Page image:** 1436–1553 × 2100 px, i.e. ≈180 dpi over the printed octavo page.
  Body text and the **count tables are legible at 1:1**; a 2–3× crop makes the
  tables unambiguous. The small concentric-ring symbols in the plates
  (e.g. Abb. 24, 25) are *not* resolvable — the ring count of a tool comes from the
  figure's numeric columns and the legend, not from the plate.
- **Verdict:** this set is good enough to *decide* every question the case asks, and
  was used to transcribe the four per-zone tables. For a printed re-publication one
  would re-scan at 300–400 dpi.

## Known traps in this source

- **Units.** Menghin's counter is *concentric rings per punched disc × number of
  discs* ("Zähler" / day-value), **not** the number of symbols. `A` (Anzahl der
  Symbole) and `SK` (Summe der Kreise) are different columns of the same table.
  Never sum a Menghin `A` with a Menghin `SK`, and never sum either with
  Schmidt-Kaler's plain counts.
- **The intercalary ("Schalt") set.** In the 2000 article the Berlin Schaltzonen
  are `{7,16,17}` everywhere (pp. 45, 71, 73, 81–83, 89); zone 5 is a *Sonderzone*
  drawn in the same grey as the Schaltzonen and *added* (as 38) for solar cycles.
  The same holds in 2010 (Abb. 23 / 61.x). The set is therefore stable; what
  moves is the **bookkeeping of the 54-month lunar value**: Menghin 2000 reaches
  1597 as `1701 − (38 + 66)`, while Wikipedia / Menghin 2010 reach the same 1597
  as `1739 − (38 + 38 + 66)`. Same number, different start. The genuinely free
  parameter is *which zone blocks are added at all* — that is Schlosser's and
  Peter-Röcher's point, and it is a real one.
- **Menghin's own summary counts do not all close.** Berlin: p. 81 of the 2000
  article gives **418** discs in 18 friezes, pp. 83 and 91 give **399**
  "Buckelscheiben" for the same 1701 rings; the table sums to 418, and the
  independently transcribed **2010 text (p. 31: "418 Buckelscheiben (Nr. 8–15) über
  18 horizontale Ornamentzonen … 1701 Kreisringe") confirms 418**. Read the 399 as
  a slip in the 2000 article; use 418. Ezelsdorf: zones 3–20 symbol counts come out
  475 / 508 / 465 from the table where the text prints 474 / 505 / 462. And the
  Avanton six-lunation row (p. 79) divides 177 by 6 while saying Schaltzone 12 was
  dropped. The **ring** sums all reproduce. `scripts/verify_menghin2000.py` prints
  these deltas as WARN; they are flagged, not forced.
- The summary on Wikipedia is a **secondary** transcription; two of Menghin's own
  states (61.2, 64.4) do not close cleanly (`scripts/verify_scenarios.py`).
- **Avanton zones 12–14 are reconstructions** from the 19th-c. Speyer plaster cast
  (dashed line in Abb. 20). Any cross-hat test on Avanton must exclude or flag them.
- **Ezelsdorf zone 21 (the rim) is a reconstruction** (Menghin's own, p. 86): the
  1737 total and the 1694 ring count depend on it. The rimless object gives 1506
  "Zeichen" / 1463 rings, zones 3–20.

## Data derived from this source

- `data/readings/menghin2000_zones.json` — the four per-zone tables (Berlin p. 45,
  Schifferstadt p. 50, Avanton p. 53, Ezelsdorf p. 61), with `_source` and the
  printed totals. Verified by `scripts/verify_menghin2000.py` (control C6).
- `data/zones.json` / `data/readings/menghin.json` — the 2010-derived Berlin table.
  `scripts/verify_menghin2000.py` cross-checks the 2000 table against it; they agree
  zone by zone.
