# Source: Menghin 2000 / 2010 — the Berlin Gold Hat ornament analysis

**What it is:** The primary measurement and the leading lunisolar hypothesis.
- W. Menghin, *Der Berliner Goldhut und die goldenen Kalendarien der alteuropäischen
  Bronzezeit*, Acta Praehistorica et Archaeologica 32 (2000), 31–108.
- W. Menghin, *Der Berliner Goldhut — Macht, Magie und Mathematik in der Bronzezeit*
  (Die Sammlungen des Museums für Vor- und Frühgeschichte 2), Regensburg 2010,
  ISBN 978-3-7954-2271-4; the ornament analysis is at pp. 47–63 and Figs 61.1–64.4.
- W. Menghin, *Goldene Kalenderhüte — Manifestationen bronzezeitlicher
  Kalenderwerke*, in: T. Springer (ed.), *Gold und Kult der Bronzezeit*
  (Germanisches Nationalmuseum Nürnberg, 2003), 221–237.

**Licence:** in copyright. Quote short passages only.

**Already on this machine?** Partly. `assets/menghin/text/` holds transcriptions

## Getting it

The pages in the repo came from the book. To obtain the whole book: Deutsche
Nationalbibliothek Frankfurt (the source cited by the project), or interlibrary
loan. No open PDF exists. The 2003 catalogue chapter may be in the
*Gold und Kult der Bronzezeit* reprint (ISBN 3-926982-95-0).

## Known traps in this source

- The **intercalary (“Schalt”) zone set moves**: Menghin 2000 uses `{5,7,16,17}`
  (54-month example `1739 − 142 = 1597`); the 2010 figures colour only `{7,16,17}`
  and put zone 5 in the solar blocks. The free parameter is unstable.
- The summary on Wikipedia is a **secondary** transcription; two of Menghin's own
  states (61.2, 64.4) do not close cleanly (`scripts/verify_scenarios.py`).
- **Units:** his unit is *concentric rings per punched disc × number of discs*
  ("day-value"), not plain symbol count. Do not confuse with Schmidt-Kaler.
