## Key content (read from the PDF)

- **The lunar year is not a constant.** It averages 354.37 d but **varies by more
  than 26 hours (> 1 day)** from year to year (his Table, 1967–2003). "Es gibt nicht
  'das' Mondjahr schlechthin, und die Zahl 354 macht nur im Mittel Sinn — nicht im
  Einzelfall!" A find with 354 marks can therefore hardly have been a running
  calendar.
- **New and full moon are unsuitable** as year indicators (new moon invisible;
  full moon barely changes phase over a day); the quarters are better but half the
  time below the horizon.
- **The Metonic rule holds only about 2/3 of the time** in his sample — "Von
  'exakt' kann somit nicht die Rede sein" (answering Menghin 2000, 90 n. 102).
- **The method critique**, aimed at May/Zumpe's Herzsprung shield and extended to
  the gold hats: it is illegitimate to select, from the many possible sums, exactly
  those that fit a pre-formed "lunar calendar" concept. He quotes Menghin's own
  admission (2000, 71) that plausible number products are to be expected "nur bei
  flexibler Handhabung der … 'Schaltzonen'".
- **Conclusion:** "die zitierten Arbeiten demonstrieren eher die Intelligenz und
  Findigkeit ihrer Autoren als die astronomischen Kenntnisse der Menschen der
  Bronzezeit." He asks that an astronomer be involved and notes that a calendar
  that only yields after long computation would have had no user.

## Known traps in this source

- It is a critique of *method*, and its sharpest example is the Herzsprung shield,
  not the gold hats; the gold-hat passage is explicitly brief ("hier nicht
  detailliert durchgeführt"). Cite it as the method critique it is.
- It attacks the *fixed-354*/lunar-year premise. It does not refute that the hats
  encode *solar* or *Metonic interval* numbers; those need the separate tests.
