# Theory: Amendola — the Schifferstadt deposit was a viewing place

**Author / date:** Luca Amendola (Heidelberg), 2021.
**Source:** `sources/amendola_2021.md` (J. Skyscape Archaeology 7, DOI
10.1558/jsa.18113).

## The claim

Instead of reading the ornament, look at *where the hat was buried*. From the
Schifferstadt find spot the horizon marked:
- sunrise at the equinox,
- sunset at the summer and winter solstices,
- the rising of the Pleiades.

That is the same functional package as the Nebra disc's location (Brocken
solstice sunset) and as several prehistoric circular enclosures — except that at
Schifferstadt the **natural** hilly horizon does the job of an artificial one.
The deposition place was, very likely, chosen for those alignments.

## Status

**Candidate.** Peer-reviewed and modern, but it concerns **Schifferstadt**, not
Berlin, and it is about the *landscape*, not the band counts. It cannot corroborate
Menghin's Berlin reading.

## What would confirm

- A full reading of the paper (currently abstract only) showing the alignments are
  precise and not horizon-hunting.
- The same measurement at Ezelsdorf-Buch and Avanton, if their find spots were
  known (Avanton's is not; Ezelsdorf's context is lost).

## What would refute

- Horizon-archaeology's usual trap: with a hilly horizon many alignments are
  available. Does the paper pre-register which events count, and correct for the
  number of candidate sightlines?
- An alternative (non-astronomical) reason for the deposition location.

## Why it matters for the replica

It is the cleanest example of an *independent* line of evidence — place, not
pattern — and it is one of the genuinely new (2021) contributions the project was
not yet aware of. Worth a slide/screen panel: "the calendar may be in the place,
not (only) in the hat."
